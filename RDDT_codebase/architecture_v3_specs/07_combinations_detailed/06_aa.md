# AA — 2 rules

Question: **"Is this inflammatory (serum amyloid A) amyloidosis?"** The simplest phenotype in the
whole system by design — one real gate, two confidence levels, one optional strengthening
modifier that is never itself a gate.

**Contributing sign IDs** (Tier 1–2, gate-eligible, AA overlay):

| Bucket | Sign IDs |
|---|---|
| INFLAMMATORY_DRIVER | `AA08:T1, AA06:T1, AA02:T1, AA01:T2, AA10:T2, AA07:T2, AA09:T2` |
| RENAL | `AA17:T1, AA16:T1, AA20:T2, AA15:T2` |

The non-gating strengthening bucket, `INFLAMMATORY_ACTIVITY` (`AA05`, `AA12`, `AA13`, `AA14`),
never appears in the table above because — by design — it can never satisfy a gate on its own. It
only ever raises the *priority* of a rule that's already passed via the two buckets above.

## AA_RULE_01 — INFLAMMATORY_DRIVER + RENAL, strengthened by INFLAMMATORY_ACTIVITY

**Plain question:** Does the patient have a credible cause of sustained inflammation **and** a
kidney phenotype that plausibly follows from it — **and, if we also have evidence the
inflammation is currently active (not just a remote/inactive diagnosis), treat this as the
strongest possible AA pattern**?

**Requires:** `TRIPLE_WITH_MODIFIER` — `INFLAMMATORY_DRIVER` Tier 1–2 **+** `RENAL` Tier 1–2,
**strengthened but not gated by** `INFLAMMATORY_ACTIVITY` Tier 1–2.

**How the modifier actually works, step by step:**
1. Check the gate first: does the patient have `INFLAMMATORY_DRIVER` Tier 1–2 *and* `RENAL`
   Tier 1–2? If either is missing, stop — this rule does not fire, no matter how much
   inflammatory-activity evidence exists.
2. If the gate passes, *then* check `INFLAMMATORY_ACTIVITY`. If it's also Tier 1–2, the rule
   fires at its **strongest priority**. If `INFLAMMATORY_ACTIVITY` is absent or weak, the rule
   still fires — just without the strength upgrade.

**Priority:** A when the activity modifier is present and Tier 1–2; a lower flavor of A (or
effectively equivalent to `AA_RULE_02`'s B, in ranking terms) when it isn't — the workbook doesn't
split this into two separate rule IDs the way ATTRwt does, so treat "driver + renal + strong
recent activity" as the top of the AA ranking and "driver + renal, activity unknown/weak" as
one notch below it, both still under the `AA_RULE_01` label.

**Output route:** `AA_REVIEW`

**Worked example, full strength:** Patient has `AA02` (known FMF, INFLAMMATORY_DRIVER Tier 1),
`AA16` (new persistent proteinuria, RENAL Tier 1), and `AA13` (persistently elevated SAA,
INFLAMMATORY_ACTIVITY Tier 1). All three present → `AA_RULE_01` fires at its strongest priority.

**Worked example, gate only:** Same patient, but no SAA/CRP/flare data on file at all (activity
bucket empty, not failed — just no data). `INFLAMMATORY_DRIVER` + `RENAL` still both satisfied →
`AA_RULE_01` still fires, just without the activity-driven strength boost.

**Worked example, gate fails:** Patient has `AA13` (very high SAA) and `AA12` (elevated CRP) but
**no** `RENAL` evidence at all. Even with strong activity evidence, the gate never opens — this
patient does not pass `AA_RULE_01`. This is the concrete illustration of "activity strengthens
priority but is NOT an independent bucket" from your original plan (§15): a patient cannot
substitute inflammatory-activity labs for the renal transition that actually defines the disease
pattern.

## AA_RULE_02 — INFLAMMATORY_DRIVER + RENAL, activity data missing/weak

**Plain question:** Functionally the same gate as `AA_RULE_01`, used specifically when there's
**no meaningful `INFLAMMATORY_ACTIVITY` signal to check at all** (as opposed to `AA_RULE_01`
firing without the boost when activity data merely doesn't push it to the top priority).

**Requires:** `INFLAMMATORY_DRIVER` Tier 1–2 + `RENAL` Tier 1–2 — identical gate to `AA_RULE_01`.

**Priority:** B.

**Output route:** `AA_REVIEW`

**Family/genetic susceptibility note:** `HEREDITARY`-bucket findings specific to AA context
(`AA03` — high-risk MEFV genotype, `AA04` — family history of AA/ESRD) can modify how this rule's
priority is reported to a reviewer, but — same rule as the activity modifier — **they can never
replace either the driver or the renal side of the gate.** A patient with `AA03` (M694V
homozygosity) and nothing else does not pass any AA rule; genotype is background risk, not organ
evidence.

**Worked example:** Patient has `AA07` (aggressive/fistulizing Crohn disease, INFLAMMATORY_DRIVER
Tier 2) and `AA15` (new microalbuminuria, RENAL Tier 2) — the gate is satisfied at Tier 2 on both
sides, and there's no CRP/SAA/flare data in the chart to check → `AA_RULE_02` fires →
`AA: PRIORITY B`.

## Why AA only needs two rules where ATTRv needed seven

ATTRv genuinely presents in structurally different ways — neuropathy-led, autonomic-led,
cardiac-led, ocular-led, genotype-led — each needing its own pairing because none of those organ
systems substitutes for another. AA's biology is much more linear: a sustained inflammatory driver
causes SAA production, which deposits and causes a renal phenotype. There's really only one causal
chain, so there's only one gate — the two rules just represent "with confirmed ongoing activity"
versus "without it," not two different disease shapes.
